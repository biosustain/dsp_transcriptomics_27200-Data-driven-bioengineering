## S. aureus — 01 Quality Control

**Q1. Look at your library-size barplot and pick the tallest and the shortest bar. Then the biological question: if a biofilm sample had twice the total counts of a planktonic sample, would that mean biofilm cells were making twice as much RNA — why or why not?**

Size factors broadly track library size: the tallest bar should land above 1.0 and the shortest below, and if all your values sit near 1.0 the libraries are balanced and there is nothing to investigate — exactly the check the Summary table asks you to record. A sample where the prediction fails is itself informative: DESeq2 uses median-of-ratios, not total counts, so a big library with a size factor near 1.0 hints that a few highly expressed genes, not overall depth, inflated its bar. For the biological question: total counts reflect sequencing depth and library preparation, not cellular RNA output. Bulk RNA-seq measures relative composition within each library, so absolute transcription per cell is invisible without spike-ins — which is precisely why normalisation must happen before any between-sample comparison.

## S. aureus — 03 Functional Enrichment

**Q1. Read 'Background (all tested)' off your own output and compare it to the size of your strain's reference genome (roughly 2,800-2,900 protein-coding genes for either strain). Where did the missing genes go, and if you put them back into the background, would your eFDR values become more or less significant? Name one kind of gene set whose verdict could flip, and explain why the smaller background is the honest one for THIS experiment.**

The missing genes were filtered out by DESeq2 as not reliably expressed in these samples, so they were never candidates for being called DE. Padding the background with them makes every observed overlap look rarer than it really is, so hypergeometric p-values and eFDRs shift toward MORE significance — flattering but wrong (the chapter's tip box calls this 'over-optimistic p-values'). Sets dominated by genes silent under both lifestyles in vitro — e.g. niche- or infection-specific pathways never switched on in these cultures — would gain apparent enrichment purely from the padding. The tested-gene background is honest because the enrichment question is 'given what we could measure here, is this overlap surprising?': the statistical universe is defined by the measurement, not by the genome.

*Principle: #5 Measurement defines reality — the statistical universe is what was measured, not what exists*

**Q2. From your ORA bar plot and tables, pick the one enriched set you trust MOST and the one you trust LEAST, and defend both choices using at least three pieces of evidence from your own table (hits in query, hits in background, eFDR) plus biological plausibility for a biofilm-planktonic switch. Would 5 hits out of a 5-gene set impress you more or less than 30 hits out of a 120-gene set, and why?**

A trustworthy set has a substantial background size, many hits, a low eFDR, and an obvious link to the lifestyle switch (e.g. ribosome or aminoacyl-tRNA sets among the planktonic-up genes, consistent with fast-growing planktonic cells). The least trustworthy hits are the tiny sets: a 5/5 overlap is close to statistically inevitable once those genes are co-regulated — a single operon can produce it — so it is a statement about set size, not biology. 30/120 is more impressive because it requires coordinated behaviour across many independently regulated genes. The chapter built collections with a minimum of 5 genes per set precisely to blunt this, and the 'spurious hits' box makes the point directly: the hypergeometric test has no notion of biological plausibility — that filter is the analyst's job.

*Principle: #4 Models are controlled simplifications — the test knows overlap counts, not biology*

**Q3. Compare 'Genes tested (background)' from the DE-loading step with 'Genes in ranked list' printed here. Genes with pvalue = 0 have -log10(p) = infinity, which cannot be placed on a finite ranking scale — the representation simply cannot express them (the 'Infinite values: 0' line in the output confirms they are gone). **Q1. Your Prepare Input chunk prints three numbers — up-regulated, down-regulated, and 'Background (all tested)'. Against a genome-wide null, anything expressed in ASM looks enriched simply because it is expressed: broad muscle, adhesion, metabolism and signalling terms appear that say nothing about dexamethasone. The genes absent from the tested set (olfactory receptors, neuronal and testis-specific genes, etc.) are not pathway-random — they cluster heavily in whole GO branches — so a genome-wide background biases the null toward 'tissue identity' findings. Restricting the background to DESeq2-tested genes (what the chapter passes as the custom background) makes the null 'genes that could have been detected in this experiment', so surviving terms reflect the treatment, not the tissue. 'Enriched more than expected by chance' has no meaning until you define chance, and the background IS that definition — changing it changes which biology the test reports as existing.

*Principle: #5 Measurement defines reality — the background choice defines the null, and the null defines what counts as a finding.*
